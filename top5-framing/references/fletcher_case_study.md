# The Fletcher Case Study — Worked Example

Source: Jason Fletcher, "How to Write a Top 5 Paper: A Case Study,"
*Mentorless Apprentice* Substack, July 2026.
<https://jasonmfletcher.substack.com/p/how-to-write-a-top-5-paper-a-case>

Summarised here in paraphrase for use as a worked example. Read the original
for Fletcher's own framing; this file exists to give the six tests a concrete
referent, not to substitute for the essay.

---

## The setup

Two papers, written at nearly the same time, on the same question: what did
NAFTA do to adult mortality in the United States?

- **Fletcher & Noghanibehambari.** Landed at the *Canadian Journal of
  Economics*.
- **Finkelstein and co-authors.** Top-5 trajectory, *New York Times*
  coverage. (Fletcher's post refers to "Finkelstein et al."; the exact
  co-author list is not confirmed in the post itself — do not cite specific
  co-authors from this file.)

The reduced-form results were close to identical. Fletcher's argument is that
the credibility of the estimate was never the differentiator. What differed
was how much work each paper asked the estimate to do.

**What each paper did:**

| | Fletcher & Noghanibehambari | Finkelstein et al. |
|---|---|---|
| Framing | NAFTA's effect on adult mortality, directly | NAFTA as one case in a broader puzzle about economic contractions |
| Question | Did NAFTA raise mortality? | Why do manufacturing job losses raise mortality when recessions may *lower* it? |
| Pathways | Employment, income, housing, disability, insurance — added side by side | One mechanism (the *composition* of job losses), pressed across settings |
| Canonical object | None | Formal welfare calculation: health costs weighed against conventional trade gains |
| Structure | Horizontal — more outcomes, no unifying thesis | Vertical — each section escalates the stakes of the previous |

The Finkelstein paper's central claim — that the composition of job losses is
what matters — survives even if you delete NAFTA from the paper. That is what
lets it organise a conversation larger than the setting.

## Scoring the papers against the six tests

This is what a completed diagnostic looks like. Note that Fletcher's paper
fails tests that have nothing to do with the quality of its identification.

### Fletcher & Noghanibehambari

| # | Test | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Deletion | **FAIL** | Remove NAFTA and no question remains — the paper *is* the setting. |
| 2 | Fact vs. explanation | **FAIL** | A new fact about one trade episode; no mechanism tested elsewhere. |
| 3 | One-sentence | **FAIL** | "NAFTA had another cost" — true, but nobody outside the subfield repeats it. |
| 4 | Vertical structure | **FAIL** | Five pathway sections sitting side by side. Reads as switching the Y variable. |
| 5 | Canonical object | **FAIL** | Estimates never enter a welfare, elasticity, or aggregation calculation. |
| 6 | Boldest responsible claim | **FAIL** | The evidence could support a trade-welfare claim; the paper declines to make it. |

### Finkelstein et al.

| # | Test | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Deletion | **PASS** | The puzzle — job losses vs. recessions — stands without NAFTA. |
| 2 | Fact vs. explanation | **PASS** | Composition of job losses is a portable mechanism, tested beyond one shock. |
| 3 | One-sentence | **PASS** | "Health costs may reverse the welfare gains from trade." |
| 4 | Vertical structure | **PASS** | Each section raises the stakes of the last, toward the welfare calculation. |
| 5 | Canonical object | **PASS** | Estimates forced into a trade-gains welfare calculation. |
| 6 | Boldest responsible claim | **PASS** | Bold intro, earned by the welfare step rather than asserted. |

**The highest-leverage change for the Fletcher paper** was not another
robustness table or a cleaner instrument. It was test 5: push the mortality
estimate into a stylized welfare calculation against the conventional gains
from trade, with caveats. That single step also fixes tests 1, 3, and 6,
because it supplies the portable claim the paper is missing.

This is the pattern to look for. **The six tests are not independent.** Adding
the canonical object usually rescues the deletion test and the one-sentence
test at the same time, because a canonical calculation is *how* a result
becomes portable. When you can only recommend one change, recommend the one
that cascades.

## Two things to carry into every review

1. **The costly fixes are cheap relative to what people usually do instead.**
   Fletcher's paper had five pathway sections. Cutting three of them and
   adding one stylized welfare calculation is less work than what was already
   done — and would have raised the ceiling. Horizontal comprehensiveness is
   often *more* effort than vertical escalation, not less.

2. **Fletcher's own diagnosis is the honest version.** He is not claiming the
   other paper is better identified, or that his was unlucky. In his words,
   evidence rarely announces its own generality — the authors have to build
   the bridge. Reviews run under this skill should be equally direct: name the
   missing bridge, don't soften it into a comment about exposition.
